# [[DuPont Decomposition (ROE)]]

**Context:** [[MUIG_AAP_MOC]] · tells apart ROEs that look identical · companion to [[ROIC vs Cost of Capital]]

> [!abstract] Quick Revision
> - **🎯 Objective:** split ROE into margin × turnover × leverage ➔ name **what drives the return**.
> - **⚠️ Key Constraint:** a 20% ROE built on **leverage** is not the same asset as a 20% ROE built on **margin** — leverage reflects balance-sheet risk, not business quality.

## 📝 Core
$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{Net profit}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{net margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total assets}}}_{\text{asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Total assets}}{\text{Equity}}}_{\text{financial leverage}}$$

| Lever | Measures | Driven by |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Net margin | how much of each ringgit of sales you keep | pricing power, cost control, mix |
| Asset turnover | how hard the asset base is worked | capital intensity, utilisation, efficiency |
| Financial leverage | how much of the asset base is borrowed | **balance-sheet risk, not business quality** |

### Same ROE, three different stories
| Business type | Margin | Turnover | Leverage | What drives the return |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Supermarket / distributor | very low | very high | low | volume and inventory speed |
| Aluminium smelter with cheap power | high | low | moderate | margin, protected by cost position |
| Bank | low spread | very low | very high | leverage — why banks are regulated |

### Worked example — Company A (Core PAT basis, RM'000)
| | Net margin | Asset turnover | Leverage | ROE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| FY24 | $885.6 / 22{,}328.8 = 3.97\%$ | $22{,}328.8 / 2{,}536.9 = 8.80\times$ | $2{,}536.9 / 1{,}267.7 = 2.00\times$ | $69.9\%$ |
| FY25 | $1{,}031.7 / 22{,}496.0 = 4.59\%$ | $22{,}496.0 / 2{,}018.0 = 11.15\times$ | $2{,}018.0 / 1{,}090.6 = 1.85\times$ | $94.6\%$ |

- **Reading it** ➔ a thin-margin distributor (cakes & delights) whose ROE comes from **extreme turnover** — asset-light, plus a small equity base after the FY25 RM1.2m dividend ➔ supermarket-type story, not a margin story.

## 🧠 Active Recall
> [!FAQ]- Two firms both report 20% ROE. What single question does DuPont answer that ROE cannot?
> > [!SUCCESS]- Answer
> > - **Short answer:** *where* the 20% comes from — margin, turnover, or borrowed money.
> > - **Why:** **Leverage term** ➔ $\frac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}$ inflates ROE with risk, not quality; a margin-driven ROE (pricing power) is more durable than a leverage-driven one.
