# [[Competitive Positioning and Economic Moat]]

**Context:** [[MUIG_AAP_MOC]] · "why can't the shop next door copy it?" · the moat is what justifies a premium multiple ([[Ratios for Shortlisting vs Evaluating]]) and a long high-ROIC period ([[ROIC vs Cost of Capital]])
**What it weighs:** whether an advantage is structurally unique, executed better, and durable.

> [!abstract] Quick Revision
> - **🎯 Test question:** does the company have something nobody else has — or is it better than everyone at an existing product — and can it **sustain** it?
> - **⚠️ Key Constraint:** the **peer list** is not just for comparing advantages — it also sets the **risk profile used for WACC** (betas, D/E) ➔ [[WACC and CAPM]].

## 📝 Core Commitments
### 1. Peer selection list
- **Start** ➔ industry classification gives the base peer set.
- **Then test it** ➔ do peers operate in different product / end-market segments? · do they carry different risk profiles — why (geography, customer base, regulatory exposure, raw-material exposure)?

### 2. Competitive positioning
- **Label it** ➔ market leader · premium positioning · differentiation.
- **Paths to success** ➔ brand strength · cost leadership · access to protected technology or resources (CFA guide).

### 3. Moat test — three criteria
| Criterion | Question | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Uniqueness** | can competitors *structurally* not replicate it? | IP, network effects, regulatory licence |
| **Relative advantage** | if shared industry-wide, is the company's **execution** superior? | quality, cost efficiency, brand |
| **Durability** | how long will it last; what erodes it? | tech disruption, new entrants, substitutes |

- **Buffett** ➔ "economic castles protected by unbreachable moats".

## 🧩 Case Application Drill
> [!QUESTION]- Case 1: F&N (F&B manufacturing). (1) Industry demand drivers that benefit it? (2) Build a peer list. (3) How is it positioned and what moat supports its leading position / premium?
> > [!SUCCESS]- Model verdict
> > - **Method first:** peers from F&B-manufacturing classification, then drop those with different end-markets (e.g. pure commodity processors) or materially different risk (geography, raw-material exposure) — the same list later feeds beta/D/E for WACC.
> > - **Positioning:** established brand leader in its home markets (label it: market leader / premium).
> > - **Moat test:** brands + vertical integration ➔ *relative advantage* (execution, brand) more than true *uniqueness*; durability depends on brand loyalty vs substitutes and input-cost shocks.
> > - **Verdict form:** name the advantage, classify it (unique vs relative), state how long it lasts and what erodes it — then the margin/growth line it supports.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- 💡 **"Strong brand" with no erosion analysis** ➔ durability is one third of the test.
- 💡 **Peers by name only** ➔ a "peer" with different segments or risk distorts both the multiple and the WACC.
