# [[Equity Research Process (Story to Line Item)]]

**Context:** [[MUIG_AAP_MOC]] · the spine of the whole programme · every later note ([[Business Model Analysis]], [[Three-Statement Model (Linkages)]], [[DCF Valuation (FCFF)]]) is one stage of this pipeline

> [!abstract] Quick Revision
> - **🎯 Objective:** turn a qualitative view into a number ➔ judgement → input assumption → three-statement forecast → valuation → target price & call.
> - **⚠️ Key Constraint:** **name the line item or drop the claim** — "strong brand", "good management", "ESG leader" are worth nothing until they move a margin, the discount rate, or the growth period.

## 📝 Core
### 1. A listed company is a mamak stall with an annual report
Offered 10% of the stall downstairs for RM50,000 — the eight questions *are* equity research:

| Question | Research area |
| :--- | :--- |
| What does it sell, and to whom? | Product & customer |
| How much does it make a year? | Revenue, margin, net profit |
| Does the cash actually show up? | Cash conversion ➔ [[Free Cash Flow (Cash vs Profit)]] |
| Why can't the shop next door copy it? | Competitive advantage ➔ [[Competitive Positioning and Economic Moat]] |
| Is the neighbourhood growing? | Industry & demand ➔ [[Industry Analysis (Headwinds and Tailwinds)]] |
| Is the owner honest and capable? | Management quality |
| What could kill it? | Risk ➔ [[Investment Risks (Risk Matrix)]] |
| Is RM50,000 a fair price? | Valuation ➔ [[Target Price and Valuation Triangulation]] |

### 2. The pipeline — every judgement must land on a line item
| Stage | Press Metal example |
| :--- | :--- |
| Qualitative judgement | "Cheap long-term hydropower locks in a cost advantage" |
| Input assumption | Cost per tonne stays flat while peers' energy costs rise |
| Three-statement forecast | Revenue, margin, capex, working capital, debt |
| Valuation | DCF and/or a peer multiple on forecast earnings |
| Target price & call | Fair value vs market price ➔ Buy / Hold / Sell |

- **Rule 1 — not in the model, not believed** ➔ a claim with no line item is decoration.
- **Rule 2 — in the model, must be defended** ➔ every assumption is a testable statement about the future: *why that number, what evidence?*

### 3. Worked mapping — Press Metal (integrated smelter, ~1.08 Mt/yr capacity, Mukah & Samalaju, Sarawak)
| Observation | Lands on | Effect on value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Long-term PPAs with Sarawak Energy fix energy cost | Cost per tonne; gross-margin floor | Raises margin **and its stability** |
| Smelting capacity largely built out | Volume growth rate; capex schedule | **Caps** growth — be honest about it |
| Aluminium sold at LME price (price taker) | $\text{Revenue} = \text{volume} \times \text{LME price} \times \text{FX}$ | **Largest single swing factor** ➔ [[Sensitivity and Scenario Analysis]] |
| Downstream extrusion integrated | Product mix; realised price above LME | Modest uplift; less pure-commodity risk |

### 4. Where the edge ("surprise") comes from
- **Informational** ➔ you know what others don't — usually unavailable, illegal past a point; legitimate form = primary research (channel checks, site visits, customers/suppliers); fades fast. **Weakest edge for a student.**
- **Analytical** ➔ same facts, better interpretation — modelling an ignored segment, decoding a "black-box" cost structure, spotting a cyclical peak that is really a structural re-rating (or vice versa). **Your primary edge.**
- **Behavioural** ➔ same facts, reading the *market's* perception (e.g. narrative premium on a founder-led stock).
- **Bursa angle** ➔ hundreds of listed names have little/no coverage and cyclicals are routinely valued on last year's earnings ➔ rich analytical + horizon edge.

### 5. The fundamentals checklist (session wrap-up)
1. What does this business sell, and to whom?
2. Does it earn more on capital than capital costs? ➔ [[ROIC vs Cost of Capital]]
3. Does the profit turn into cash?
4. Why can't a competitor take it away?
5. What does today's price already assume?
6. What do I believe that the price does not — and what would prove me wrong? ➔ [[Investment Thesis]]

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- 💡 **Adjective analysis** ➔ listing qualities ("market leader", "ESG leader") without the margin/WACC/growth line they change.
- 💡 **Precision ≠ accuracy** ➔ a model to two decimals is still only as good as its assumptions; test it ([[Sensitivity and Scenario Analysis]]).

## 🧠 Active Recall
> [!FAQ]- An analyst writes "management is excellent" in the thesis. What must happen before this earns credit?
> > [!SUCCESS]- Answer
> > - **Short answer:** it must show up as a number in the model and be defended with evidence.
> > - **Why:** **Story → line item** ➔ e.g. better capital allocation ➔ higher ROIC / margin, or lower risk ➔ lower discount rate, or longer competitive advantage ➔ longer growth period; otherwise drop the claim.

> [!FAQ]- Which of the three sources of edge is realistic for a student on Bursa, and why?
> > [!SUCCESS]- Answer
> > - **Short answer:** analytical edge.
> > - **Why:** **Informational** edge is mostly unavailable/illegal and decays; **analytical** edge needs only public filings + better interpretation, and many Bursa names are uncovered or mis-valued on trailing cyclical earnings.
