# [[Free Cash Flow (Cash vs Profit)]]

**Context:** [[MUIG_AAP_MOC]] · why professionals trust cash over profit · the valuation version is FCFF in [[DCF Valuation (FCFF)]]

> [!abstract] Quick Revision
> - **🎯 Objective:** $\text{FCF} = \text{Net cash from operating activities} - \text{Capex}$ ➔ the only pool that can pay dividends, buy back shares, repay debt, or fund acquisitions without borrowing.
> - **⚠️ Key Constraint:** **"Profit is an opinion. Cash is a fact."** — but negative FCF is a *question*, not automatically a red flag (TNB).

## 📝 Core
### 1. Why profit can mislead
- **Revenue booked before cash** ➔ a sale on 120-day credit is profit today, cash next year — or never if the customer fails.
- **Depreciation is an estimate** ➔ stretch the useful life and profit rises with nothing changing in the real world.
- **Capitalising costs** ➔ moving spend from the income statement to the balance sheet improves margins on paper.
- **Profit ignores the cash needed to stay alive** ➔ a growing manufacturer must fund inventory, receivables and replacement machinery before it can pay you.

### 2. Quick diagnostic
- **5-year test** ➔ compare **cumulative net profit** with **cumulative FCF** over five years ➔ if profit keeps arriving and cash never does, the accounting is doing work the business isn't.
- **Company A check (RM'000)** ➔ FY22–FY25 cumulative PAT $= 54.6 + 288.7 + 854.3 + 1{,}022.8 = 2{,}220.4$; cumulative FCF $= (176.8 - 150.0) + (201.7 - 256.8) + (914.5 - 324.3) + (736.1 - 0) = 1{,}298.0$ ➔ roughly 58% conversion; the gap is FY22–24 capex and the FY24–25 receivable build.

### 3. Case — Tenaga Nasional (FY2025)
| Net profit | Operating cash flow | Capex | FCF |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| RM4.77bn | RM15.8bn | RM16.3bn | **−RM0.44bn** |

- **Red flag? Not necessarily** ➔ regulated utility earns an allowed return on its regulated asset base ➔ spending RM16bn to enlarge that base **is** the business model working.
- **But it changes the questions** ➔ if dividends exceed FCF, what funds them — debt or asset sales? · does the allowed return on new capex exceed the cost of capital? · how much further can gearing rise before the credit rating moves?
- **Data caveat** ➔ FY2023 OCF (RM32.2bn) was inflated by a large recovery of fuel-cost receivables ➔ one-off swings distort single-year FCF.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- 💡 **FCF ≠ FCFF** ➔ this note's FCF is *CFO − capex* (after interest and tax paid); DCF uses FCFF $= \text{NOPAT} + \text{D\&A} - \Delta\text{NWC} - \text{Capex}$ (before financing).
- 💡 **Single-year verdicts** ➔ a capex-heavy year or a receivable recovery can flip FCF sign; use multi-year sums.

## 🧠 Active Recall
> [!FAQ]- TNB has RM4.8bn profit but negative FCF. Give the argument that this is fine, and the three questions it forces.
> > [!SUCCESS]- Answer
> > - **Short answer:** capex grows the regulated asset base on which TNB earns its allowed return, so negative FCF is the growth model — but check dividend funding, return vs cost of capital, and gearing headroom.
> > - **Why:** **Regulated growth** ➔ earnings $\propto$ RAB $\times$ allowed return; value is created only if allowed return $>$ cost of capital, and debt-funded dividends are bounded by the credit rating.
