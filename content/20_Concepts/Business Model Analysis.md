# [[Business Model Analysis]]

**Context:** [[MUIG_AAP_MOC]] · "A stock as a business — do I understand how it earns money now and how it will in future?" · feeds the revenue drivers in [[Forecasting Drivers (Revenue and Cost)]]
**What it weighs:** *how* the company operates to make money, not just *what* it does.

> [!abstract] Quick Revision
> - **🎯 Test question:** what are the revenue streams, what drives each one, and where does the company sit in the industry value chain?
> - **⚠️ Key Constraint:** **business description ≠ business model** — description = what the company does; model = how it operates to make money.

## 📝 Core Commitments
### 1. Business model components
1. **Core operations** ➔ revenue sources / product segments · value chain.
2. **Target market / customers** ➔ target industry · geographical segments.
3. **Revenue mechanics / cost management** ➔ revenue drivers · cost-reducing models/frameworks.

### 2. Three questions that drive the analysis
- **How does it generate revenue?** ➔ main revenue streams + core drivers of each (each driver carries its own risks and opportunities).
- **Where does it lie in the industry value chain?** ➔ every industry has its own upstream → midstream → downstream; position decides margins, bargaining power and risk.
- **Anything special on a standalone basis (USP)?** ➔ property developer: land-acquisition strategy · service-based tech: strategy to increase traction · manufacturer: technological capabilities / R&D products.

### 3. Why segment & geography splits matter
- **Geographical split** ➔ points to which country-specific macro factors to study.
- **Screening** ➔ segment mix tells you which peers are truly comparable ➔ [[Competitive Positioning and Economic Moat]].

## 🧩 Case Application Drill
> [!QUESTION]- Case 1: Fraser & Neave Holdings (FNHB). Annual Report 2025: revenue by operating segment FY25 = F&B Malaysia 57% / F&B Indochina 43% (FY24 56% / 44%); by geography FY25 = South East Asia 92%, Middle East & Africa 4%, Greater China 3%, others 1% (FY24 94/2/3/1). Value chain described as vertically integrated. Answer the four discussion questions.
> > [!SUCCESS]- Model verdict
> > - **Revenue / product segments:** two F&B segments (Malaysia, Indochina) carry ~100% of revenue across a multi-category brand portfolio (AR p.28) — property/others immaterial.
> > - **What drives stable earnings:** staple consumer F&B (non-discretionary demand) + established brands + scale in two home markets.
> > - **Value-chain position:** vertically integrated (sourcing → production → distribution/marketing) ➔ a potential **moat** (cost, quality control) **but** exposes it to more of the chain's risks (input costs, capacity utilisation).
> > - **Target market / where:** mass-market consumers in SEA (92%) ➔ study Malaysian and Thai/Indochina macro; ME&A growing from 2% to 4% is the export angle to watch.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- 💡 **Copying the "About us" page** ➔ that's the description; marks go to drivers, value-chain position and mechanics.
- 💡 **Treating integration as pure upside** ➔ vertical integration is both an advantage and a concentration of risk.
