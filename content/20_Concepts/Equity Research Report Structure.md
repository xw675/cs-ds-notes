# [[Equity Research Report Structure]]

**Context:** [[MUIG_AAP_MOC]] · what goes in each section of a report (CFA Institute "Equity Research Report Essentials" + Lesson 1) · competition limits and marks live in [[CFA Institute Research Challenge (Rules and Scoring)]]

> [!abstract] Quick Revision
> - **🎯 Objective:** a report answers *Buy or Sell?* — what stands out, what fuels it, expected performance, intrinsic value ➔ qualitative analysis → quantitative analysis → overall verdict.
> - **⚠️ Key Constraint:** if advising a trade, state **why the stock is mispriced** — what the market is not discounting, and **what will prompt it to re-price** ➔ [[Investment Thesis]].

## 📝 Core
### 1. Analysis flow (Lesson 1)
- **Qualitative** ➔ company analysis · industry & competitive analysis · management quality and/or ESG.
- **Quantitative** ➔ accounting & earnings-quality analysis → financial analysis → prospective analysis (forecast) → valuation & investment risks.
- **Overall** ➔ executive summary & re-evaluation of the investment thesis.

### 2. Section-by-section contents
| Section | Must contain | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Basic information** (header) | ticker, primary exchange, sector/industry, recommendation, current price, market cap, target price | also flag **liquidity**, a small **float** (excludes insiders/restricted shares), **major shareholders** |
| **Business description** | products/services; company economics; key drivers of revenue and expenses | [[Business Model Analysis]] |
| **Industry overview & competitive positioning** | industry dynamics, peer group, Porter's Five Forces; capacity, pricing, distribution, share stability; the moat | [[Industry Analysis (Headwinds and Tailwinds)]] · [[Competitive Positioning and Economic Moat]] |
| **Investment summary** | brief description, recent developments, earnings forecast, valuation summary, recommended action | [[Investment Thesis]] |
| **Valuation** | absolute (DCF) **and** relative (P/S, P/E, P/CF, P/B); use **more than one model** | [[Target Price and Valuation Triangulation]] |
| **Financial analysis** | historical performance + forecast; earnings quality from the **footnotes** | [[Three-Statement Model (Linkages)]] · [[Core Earnings (Normalising Non-recurring Items)]] |
| **Investment risks** | operational, financial, regulatory, legal | [[Investment Risks (Risk Matrix)]] |
| **ESG** | environmental, social, governance management | [[ESG in Equity Research]] |

### 3. Financial-analysis traps named in the guide
- **Distortions to look for** ➔ nonrecurring events, off-balance-sheet financing, income & reserve recognition, depreciation policies.
- **Cyclicals** ➔ projecting forward from the **top or bottom of a cycle** is a common mistake.
- **Industry-specific ratios** ➔ proven reserves/share (oil), revenue/subscriber (telco/cable), revenue/available room (hotels).
- **Automatic red flags** ➔ auditor's **qualified opinion**; **material weakness** in internal control over financial reporting.

### 4. Careers that use this skill (Lesson 1)
- Investment banking · M&A · corporate finance · asset/fund management.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- 💡 **One valuation model** ➔ outputs vary; triangulate absolute with relative.
- 💡 **Taking filings at face value** ➔ "financial results are commonly manipulated to portray firms in the most favourable light" — read the notes.

## 🧠 Active Recall
> [!FAQ]- What two things must the investment summary explain when recommending a Buy?
> > [!SUCCESS]- Answer
> > - **Short answer:** why the stock is mispriced, and what will make the market re-price it.
> > - **Why:** **Mispricing + catalyst** ➔ an undervaluation with no trigger can persist indefinitely; the thesis must say what the market is not discounting *and* the event that closes the gap.
